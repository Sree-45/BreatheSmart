# Pregnancy and Air Quality

## Why pregnant women are a sensitive group
Pregnancy raises baseline ventilation (more air breathed per minute) and shifts the immune and oxidative-stress systems, so a pregnant person inhales and absorbs more pollutant for the same outdoor concentration. Critically, inhaled fine particles (PM2.5) can cross the placenta, exposing a rapidly developing fetus during windows when organs and the brain are forming. Both mother and fetus are therefore considered higher-risk than the general population.

## Documented risks of PM2.5 exposure in pregnancy
- **Preterm birth** — elevated PM2.5 in pregnancy is associated with roughly a 19% higher risk of preterm birth, with the greatest risk from high third-trimester exposure.
- **Low birth weight and reduced fetal growth**, and an increased risk of stillbirth in the highest-exposure studies.
- **Gestational diabetes** — risk rises measurably with higher average PM2.5 across preconception and the first trimester.
- **Hypertensive disorders of pregnancy (including pre-eclampsia)** are more common with higher particulate exposure.
- **Fetal brain development** may be affected by maternal PM2.5 exposure.

## Recommendations by AQI level
**AQI ≤ 50 (Good):** Normal activity. A good time for outdoor walking and prenatal exercise.

**AQI 51-100 (Moderate):** Generally fine. Prefer low-traffic routes and parks over busy roadsides; schedule longer outdoor activity for cleaner morning hours.

**AQI 101-150 (Unhealthy for Sensitive Groups):**
- Reduce prolonged or heavy outdoor exertion; take more breaks.
- Avoid arterial roads and rush-hour traffic, where PM2.5 and NO2 concentrate.
- Consider a well-fitted mask for longer unavoidable outings.
- Keep windows closed during local pollution or smoke events and run indoor HEPA filtration.

**AQI 151-200 (Unhealthy):**
- Move exercise indoors to a filtered space.
- Wear a well-fitted N95/FFP2 for unavoidable outdoor time when PM2.5 dominates.
- Minimise time outdoors, especially in the third trimester.

**AQI 201-300 (Very Unhealthy):**
- Stay indoors with windows closed and a HEPA air cleaner running.
- Avoid all outdoor exertion.
- Contact your obstetric provider if you notice reduced fetal movement, headache/visual changes (possible pre-eclampsia), or breathing difficulty.

**AQI 301+ (Hazardous):**
- Remain in a HEPA-filtered clean room; avoid all outdoor outings.
- N95 only for unavoidable exposure (e.g., evacuation).
- Seek medical care promptly for any concerning symptom — breathlessness, chest pain, severe headache, or decreased fetal movement.

## Daily best practices during pregnancy
- Check the AQI before outdoor plans and favour cleaner times of day and low-traffic areas.
- Run a HEPA air purifier in the bedroom; keep the home strictly smoke-free (avoid secondhand smoke, which compounds PM2.5 harm).
- Stay hydrated and maintain prenatal care; report new or worsening symptoms early.
- A sealed N95/FFP2 greatly reduces inhaled PM2.5; surgical and cloth masks do not.
