# Children and Air Quality

## Why children are more vulnerable
Children are not small adults. Their respiratory and immune systems are still developing, they breathe faster (higher minute ventilation per kilogram of body weight), they spend more time outdoors at peak pollution hours (school recess, sports), and they breathe nearer to ground level where exhaust pollutants are more concentrated. Mouth-breathing during play bypasses the nose's natural filtration. The cumulative result is that children typically receive 2–3x the per-kilogram dose of an adult on the same day.

Long-term consequences of childhood air pollution exposure include:
- Permanent reduction in maximum attained lung function.
- Higher lifetime asthma incidence.
- Reduced cognitive development scores in studies of urban children.
- Increased risk of childhood obesity and metabolic dysfunction (under investigation but consistent across studies).
- Increased respiratory infections (pneumonia, bronchiolitis).

## Pollutants of particular concern
- **PM2.5** drives long-term lung development effects.
- **Ozone** is the leading acute trigger for asthma exacerbations in children, particularly during summer afternoons.
- **NO2** from traffic is associated with reduced lung development and increased asthma incidence in children attending schools near busy roads.

## Recommendations by AQI level for children

**AQI ≤ 50:** Unrestricted outdoor play. Encourage active outdoor time.

**AQI 51–100:** Generally fine. Children with severe asthma or recent ED visits may want to reschedule strenuous afternoon sports.

**AQI 101–150 (Unhealthy for Sensitive Groups):**
- Reduce prolonged outdoor activity. Recess can continue but reduce sustained running.
- Reschedule outdoor PE and sports practices to morning when ozone is lower.
- Children with asthma: carry rescue inhaler to school, notify the school nurse.
- Consider indoor recess for the youngest children (under 6).

**AQI 151–200 (Unhealthy):**
- Move PE and recess indoors.
- Cancel outdoor sports practices and games.
- Children with asthma, congenital heart disease, or cystic fibrosis should not be outdoors for more than brief transitions.
- N95 masks fit poorly on children under 8 and should not be relied upon as a primary protection.

**AQI 201–300 (Very Unhealthy):**
- Schools should cancel all outdoor activities.
- Run HEPA filtration in classrooms if available.
- Keep children indoors with windows closed.
- Watch for symptoms: cough, wheezing, complaints of chest tightness, fatigue, headache.

**AQI 301+ (Hazardous):**
- Schools should consider remote operation if HVAC and filtration are insufficient.
- Children with chronic respiratory or cardiac conditions should be confined to a HEPA-filtered room.
- Seek medical care for any asthma symptoms not relieved by rescue inhaler within 15 minutes.

## Daily practices for parents and schools
- Check the AQI before school dismissal time when planning afterschool activities.
- For children with asthma, maintain controller therapy compliance and ensure the school has a current asthma action plan.
- Bedroom HEPA filtration accelerates recovery between school days and improves sleep quality.
- Avoid school routes that pass directly along arterial roads when possible.
- Vehicle idling at school drop-off zones substantially raises near-school NO2 and PM2.5; advocate for no-idling policies.
- Encourage indoor active play options (climbing gyms, indoor sports facilities) during multi-day pollution episodes.
