# COPD and Air Quality

## Why people with COPD are at elevated risk
Chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD) leaves the airways chronically inflamed, narrowed, and with reduced reserve. Inhaled particulate matter travels deep into the lung's air sacs where it irritates and corrodes tissue, increases airway inflammation, raises mucus production, and lowers lung function. The result is more frequent and more severe exacerbations — flare-ups that drive rescue-inhaler use, oral steroid courses, emergency department visits, and hospital admissions. The finest particles (PM2.5) cross from the lungs into the bloodstream, adding stroke and heart-attack risk on top of the respiratory burden, which matters because many COPD patients also have cardiovascular disease.

## Pollutants of concern, ranked
1. **PM2.5 (fine particulate matter)** — the dominant driver of COPD exacerbations and hospitalisations. Penetrates to the alveoli and into the bloodstream; effects begin within 24-72 hours of a spike. No safe lower threshold.
2. **PM10 / coarse dust** — aggravates the airways; most relevant during dust storms, wildfire smoke, and near construction.
3. **Ozone (O3)** — highly reactive; irritates airways and reduces lung function, worst on hot, sunny afternoons.
4. **NO2 and SO2** — traffic- and combustion-related; worsen existing COPD and can trigger bronchoconstriction.

## Recommendations by AQI level
**AQI ≤ 50 (Good):** Normal activity. A good day for gentle outdoor exercise and pulmonary-rehab walks.

**AQI 51-100 (Moderate):** Generally fine. If you are highly sensitive, keep rescue medication handy and consider shifting strenuous activity to the cooler morning hours when ozone is lower.

**AQI 101-150 (Unhealthy for Sensitive Groups):** Treat this as a meaningful threshold for COPD.
- Reduce prolonged or heavy outdoor exertion; take frequent breaks.
- Keep quick-relief (rescue) inhalers with you at all times.
- Run a HEPA air cleaner indoors and keep windows closed.
- Watch for increased breathlessness, cough, or sputum changes — early signs to step up therapy per your action plan.

**AQI 151-200 (Unhealthy):**
- Avoid prolonged outdoor exertion; move activity indoors.
- Wear a well-fitted N95/FFP2 mask for unavoidable outdoor time when PM2.5 or PM10 dominates.
- Pre-medicate if your physician has prescribed it; increase symptom monitoring.
- Have your oral corticosteroid/antibiotic "rescue pack" accessible if your action plan includes one.

**AQI 201-300 (Very Unhealthy):**
- Stay indoors with windows closed and a HEPA cleaner running; create a clean room if whole-home filtration is unavailable.
- Avoid all outdoor exertion.
- Have a low threshold to contact your clinician; do not wait until symptoms are severe.

**AQI 301+ (Hazardous):**
- Treat as a respiratory emergency, especially with prior exacerbations or hospitalisations.
- Remain in filtered air and limit movement to reduce ventilation.
- N95 only for unavoidable outdoor exposure (e.g., evacuation).
- Seek emergency care for severe breathlessness, confusion, bluish lips/fingertips, or symptoms unrelieved by usual medication.

## Daily best practices for COPD + air pollution
- Check the AQI (e.g., AirNow) before going out; plan errands and exercise for cleaner hours.
- Keep your home smoke-free: no indoor smoking, fireplaces, or candles; use exhaust fans while cooking.
- Run a HEPA air purifier in the bedroom; clean and mop regularly so settled PM is not re-suspended.
- Maintain controller-medication compliance even on good-air days, and keep rescue inhalers within reach 24/7.
- A properly sealed N95/FFP2 markedly reduces inhaled PM2.5; surgical and cloth masks do not.
- Avoid arterial roads and idling traffic, where NO2 and PM2.5 concentrate.
