# Asthma and Air Quality

## Why asthma patients are at elevated risk
People with asthma have airways that are chronically inflamed and hyperresponsive. Inhaled particulate matter, ozone, NO2, and SO2 trigger bronchoconstriction, increase mucus production, and accelerate airway remodelling. Even short exposures to elevated pollutant concentrations can precipitate an asthma exacerbation requiring rescue inhaler use, emergency department visits, or hospitalization. Studies consistently find that asthma-related ED visits rise within 24–72 hours of pollution spikes.

## Pollutants of concern, ranked
1. **Ozone (O3)** — the single strongest acute trigger for asthmatics. A 0.12 ppm 1-hour exposure produces measurable lung function decline and symptoms in most asthma patients. Ozone peaks on hot, sunny afternoons.
2. **PM2.5** — drives long-term airway inflammation and short-term exacerbations. Effects scale roughly linearly with concentration; there is no safe lower threshold.
3. **NO2** — chronic exposure increases asthma incidence in children. Short-term exposure aggravates existing asthma. Mostly traffic-related.
4. **SO2** — produces bronchoconstriction in asthmatics within minutes. Most relevant near coal-fired plants and metal smelters.

## Recommendations by AQI level
**AQI ≤ 50 (Good):** Normal activity. Good day for outdoor exercise.

**AQI 51–100 (Moderate):** Generally fine; if you are highly sensitive to ozone, consider rescheduling intense outdoor exercise to morning hours.

**AQI 101–150 (Unhealthy for Sensitive Groups):**
- Reduce prolonged or heavy outdoor exertion.
- Carry rescue inhaler at all times.
- If exercising outdoors, use a 10-minute warm-up and stop at the first sign of wheezing, cough, or chest tightness.
- Schedule running, cycling, and field sports for early morning when ozone is lowest.

**AQI 151–200 (Unhealthy):**
- Avoid prolonged outdoor exertion. Move workouts indoors.
- Use a properly fitted N95/FFP2 for unavoidable outdoor time when PM2.5 dominates.
- Pre-medicate per your asthma action plan if your physician has prescribed pre-exercise inhalation.
- Increase peak-flow monitoring frequency. A drop >20% from personal best warrants stepping up therapy.

**AQI 201–300 (Very Unhealthy):**
- Stay indoors with windows closed. Run a HEPA air cleaner sized to the room.
- Skip outdoor exercise entirely. Use a stationary bike or treadmill indoors.
- Keep oral corticosteroid course (if prescribed in your action plan) accessible.
- Have a low threshold to seek medical care; do not wait until symptoms are severe.

**AQI 301+ (Hazardous):**
- Treat as a respiratory emergency for asthmatics with prior hospitalizations or recent exacerbations.
- Stay in a HEPA-filtered room. Limit movement to reduce ventilation.
- N95 only for unavoidable outdoor exposure (e.g., evacuation).
- Seek emergency care for any of: persistent wheeze unresponsive to two rescue inhaler doses, peak flow <50% of personal best, difficulty speaking in full sentences, lips or fingertips turning blue.

## Daily best practices for asthma + air pollution
- Check the AQI before outdoor activities. Most cities publish hourly forecasts.
- Keep rescue inhaler within reach 24/7.
- Maintain controller medication compliance even on good-air days.
- Avoid arterial roads and idling traffic — NO2 and PM2.5 are concentrated within a few hundred metres of busy roads.
- Run a HEPA filter in the bedroom; sleep restoration accelerates airway recovery.
- N95 masks reduce inhaled PM2.5 by 95% when sealed properly. Surgical and cloth masks do not.
- Vacuum and dust regularly indoors; outdoor PM2.5 settles into household dust over time.
