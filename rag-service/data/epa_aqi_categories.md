# US EPA Air Quality Index (AQI) Categories and Recommended Actions

The US EPA's AQI is a 0–500 scale that translates pollutant concentrations into a single number with associated health categories. India's National Air Quality Index (NAQI) uses a similar 0–500 structure with comparable categories. Both are designed to communicate health risk to the public.

## Category 1: Good (AQI 0–50)
Air quality is satisfactory, and air pollution poses little or no risk. No precautions required for any group.

## Category 2: Moderate (AQI 51–100)
Air quality is acceptable. Unusually sensitive people — those with severe asthma, recent cardiac events, or pulmonary fibrosis — may experience mild symptoms with prolonged outdoor exertion. General population unaffected.

**Actions:** Sensitive individuals should monitor symptoms. Carry rescue inhaler if asthmatic. No restrictions for the general population.

## Category 3: Unhealthy for Sensitive Groups (AQI 101–150)
Members of sensitive groups may experience health effects. The general public is less likely to be affected. Sensitive groups include children (under 18), elderly (over 65), pregnant women, and people with asthma, COPD, heart disease, or diabetes.

**Actions for sensitive groups:**
- Reduce prolonged or heavy outdoor exertion.
- Take more breaks during outdoor activity.
- Reschedule strenuous outdoor exercise to early morning hours when ozone is lower.
- People with asthma should keep quick-relief medicine handy.
- Children should reduce outdoor recess and PE during peak hours.

## Category 4: Unhealthy (AQI 151–200)
Some members of the general public may experience health effects; sensitive groups will experience more serious effects.

**Actions for sensitive groups:** Avoid prolonged or heavy outdoor exertion. Move activities indoors or reschedule.
**Actions for everyone:** Reduce prolonged or heavy outdoor exertion. Take more breaks during outdoor activity. Consider an N95/FFP2 mask for unavoidable outdoor time, especially when PM2.5 is the dominant pollutant.

## Category 5: Very Unhealthy (AQI 201–300)
Health alert: the risk of health effects is increased for everyone. Emergency conditions are likely for sensitive groups.

**Actions for sensitive groups:** Avoid all physical activity outdoors. Move activities indoors or reschedule to a day with better air quality.
**Actions for everyone:** Avoid prolonged or heavy outdoor exertion. Reschedule outdoor activities to a time when air quality is better. Run HEPA filtration indoors. Keep windows closed.

## Category 6: Hazardous (AQI 301+)
Health warning of emergency conditions: everyone is more likely to be affected.

**Actions for everyone:**
- Avoid all physical activity outdoors.
- Sensitive groups should remain indoors and keep activity levels low.
- Run HEPA-grade air purifiers; create a clean room if whole-house filtration is not available.
- N95/FFP2 masks for any unavoidable outdoor exposure.
- Seek medical attention for chest pain, shortness of breath, persistent cough, or wheezing not relieved by usual medications.
- Schools and daycares should cancel outdoor activities and consider remote operation.

## Pollutant-specific notes
- When **PM2.5** drives the AQI, indoor HEPA filtration and N95 masks are the most effective interventions.
- When **ozone (O3)** drives the AQI, indoor air is usually unaffected — staying indoors or shifting activity to morning is sufficient. HEPA filters do not remove ozone; activated carbon partially does.
- When **PM10** dominates (e.g. dust storms), a properly fitted N95 is highly effective for outdoor time.
- When **NO2** dominates (heavy traffic), avoid arterial roads during rush hour; ventilation routes through quieter streets reduce exposure substantially.
